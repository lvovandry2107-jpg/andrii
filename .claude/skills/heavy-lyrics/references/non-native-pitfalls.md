# Typical issues for Russian-speaking lyricists

Explain these in Russian when you correct them.

## Articles (a / the / none)
- "I am lost in dark" → "I'm lost in the dark".
- Lyrics may drop articles for rhythm ("Blood on floor" is acceptable as a stylistic fragment only if it's clearly intentional and short). Say when a dropped article sounds like an error vs. a style choice.

## Tenses
- "I am living here since years" → "I've been living here for years".
- "When you will leave" → "When you leave".
- Present perfect for results/consequences: "You have broken me" (more weight) vs. "You broke me" (more direct — often better in lyrics).

## Calques (word-for-word from Russian)
| Calque | Natural |
|--------|---------|
| "my soul is hurting" | "my soul aches" / "it hurts" |
| "I'm tired from you" | "I'm tired of you" / "I'm sick of you" |
| "take a decision" | "make a decision" |
| "it doesn't make sense to fight" (ok) vs. "there is no sense to fight" | "there's no point in fighting" |
| "I will not forgive you never" | "I will never forgive you" (no double negatives unless deliberately slangy) |
| "in the end of the road" | "at the end of the road" |
| "rise up from knees" | "get up off my knees" / "rise from my knees" |
| "close the eyes" | "close your eyes" (possessives where Russian omits) |
| "my world is broken to pieces" | "my world fell to pieces" |

## Word stress
Check dictionary stress, e.g. "deVOUR", "beTRAY", "conTROL", "aLONE", "deSTROY", "REbel (noun)" vs "reBEL (verb)", "PREsent (noun)" vs "preSENT (verb)".

## Register
- Avoid bookish words that sound like a translation app ("whilst", "thus", "henceforth", "perish" — unless going for epic metal).
- Contractions sound natural: "I'm, you're, can't, won't, I'll".
- Slang should match the band's scene; don't mix British and American slang randomly.

## Prepositions
- "Afraid from" → "afraid of"; "depend from" → "depend on"; "angry on you" → "angry at/with you"; "think about/of"; "in the night" → "at night" (unless "in the night" is poetic).

## Pronunciation traps on vocals
- "th" (the, breathe, death) — fine to keep, but avoid stacking many in one fast screamed line.
- "w" vs "v" ("vow / wow"), long vs short vowels ("leave / live", "sheep / ship") can change meaning — point out if a word may be misheard.
